History of
Saudi Arabia

c. 3000 BCE – 600 CE

Ancient Arabia

Ancient Arabia

The Arabian Peninsula was home to communities and kingdoms such as the Thamud and Nabataeans. Trade routes connected Arabia with Asia and the Mediterranean, while places such as Hegra became important centres of trade and culture.

610 – 632 CE

The Rise of Islam

The Rise of Islam

In 610 CE, Prophet Muhammad began receiving the revelations of the Qur'an in Makkah. Islam spread throughout Arabia, while Makkah and Madinah became the two holiest cities in Islam.

c. 750 – 1258 CE

Islamic Golden Age

Islamic Golden Age

Scholars across the Islamic world made major advances in mathematics, medicine, astronomy, science and literature. Makkah and Madinah also remained important centres of Islamic learning, pilgrimage and culture.

1727 – 1818

First Saudi State

First Saudi State

The First Saudi State was established in 1727, with Diriyah as its capital. It expanded across much of the Arabian Peninsula before being defeated in 1818 by an Ottoman-Egyptian campaign.

1824 – 1891

Second Saudi State

Second Saudi State

The Second Saudi State was established in 1824 and centred on Riyadh. Internal conflicts and struggles over leadership weakened the state, which eventually collapsed in 1891.

1902 – 1932

Unification

Unification

Abdulaziz Ibn Saud recaptured Riyadh in 1902 and spent the following decades bringing different regions under his rule. On 23 September 1932, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia was officially established.

1938 – Today

Oil & Modern Saudi Arabia

Modern Saudi Arabia

Bulk amounts of oil was found near Dammam in 1938, changing Saudi Arabia's economy. Today, the Kingdom continues to modernise while preserving its historical heritage through events such as Vision 2030.
